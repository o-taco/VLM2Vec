02Calibration: precision and coverage per tapped depth

The calibration split (2,559 rows, image-disjoint from training) fits the lowest agreement-probability threshold at each depth that keeps precision ≥90% — i.e. among rows the probe is willing to route at that depth, at least 90% actually match the depth-28 answer. Coverage is the fraction of the calibration set that clears each depth's own threshold.

Four tapped depths only — not a continuous 1–28 sweep (the deck's page 8/22 curves; out of scope for this phase, see below). By depth 20 essentially every calibration row already agrees with the depth-28 answer, which is why the routed eval rarely needs to go past depth ~14 on average.

03What this does and doesn't establish

  • Confirms the mechanism works end-to-end: LoRA r=64 on the 28 language layers only (vision tower verified frozen), FiLM-conditioned shared trunk, joint answer + agreement heads through one optimizer, calibrated exit thresholds — all trained and evaluated with no shortcuts against the architecture in pages 3–6 / 18–19.
  • Single-dataset training pool, not the deck's joint 5-dataset pool. A-OKVQA-only training happens to land close to the deck's numbers on A-OKVQA itself, but says nothing about transfer to the other 9 MMEB datasets in the deck's Results tables (pages 7–8) — that needs the full pool.
  • Image-disjoint splitting is approximate: A-OKVQA's HF release has no image-id column, so answer-fit / confidence-fit / calibration are split by row, not a deduplicated image key. Most A-OKVQA images carry one question, so this should track the deck's stricter rule closely, but isn't a verified exact match.
